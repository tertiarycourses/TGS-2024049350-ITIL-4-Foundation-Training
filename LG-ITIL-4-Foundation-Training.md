## Course Goal

This course helps learners understand ITIL 4 Foundation concepts and apply them to everyday IT service management situations. The labs use a practical service desk and digital service scenario so learners can connect ITIL vocabulary with real work: managing incidents, fulfilling service requests, enabling changes, improving service levels, and co-creating value with stakeholders.

## Prerequisites

- Basic computer literacy.
- Interest in IT service management, service desk, IT operations, support, business analysis, project work, or digital transformation.
- No prior ITIL certification is required.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, learners should be able to:

- Explain key ITIL 4 service management concepts.
- Distinguish value, output, outcome, cost, risk, utility, and warranty.
- Apply the seven ITIL guiding principles to scenarios.
- Describe the four dimensions of service management.
- Explain the ITIL service value system and service value chain.
- Apply core ITIL practices including incident management, problem management, service request management, service desk, change enablement, service level management, and continual improvement.
- Use ITIL language to improve service conversations and decisions.
- Prepare for ITIL 4 Foundation exam-style questions.

```text
Organization: BrightDesk Services
Service: Employee Digital Workplace
Users: Staff, managers, IT support, HR, finance, vendors
Main service channels: Service portal, email, chat, phone, monitoring alerts
Business goal: Keep employees productive through reliable IT services
```

## Lab Working Method

Use this method in each lab:

1. Read the scenario.
2. Identify the ITIL concept or practice being tested.
3. Write the decision in ITIL terms.
4. Explain how the decision supports value.
5. Record any confusing terms in your glossary.
6. Answer the checkpoint questions.

## Exam Mindset

For ITIL 4 Foundation:

1. Read the question carefully and identify the exact ITIL term.
2. Eliminate answers that sound practical but do not match ITIL definitions.
3. Prefer value-focused, collaborative, iterative, and practical answers.
4. Remember that ITIL is adaptable guidance, not a rigid procedure.
5. Watch for common confusion pairs:

```text
Output vs outcome
Utility vs warranty
Incident vs problem
Service request vs incident
Change enablement vs release management
Guiding principle vs practice
Service value system vs service value chain
```

## Final Exam Readiness Checklist

Before attempting ITIL 4 Foundation, confirm that you can:

- Define service, product, service offering, output, outcome, cost, risk, utility, and warranty.
- Explain value co-creation.
- List and apply the seven guiding principles.
- Explain the four dimensions of service management.
- Explain the ITIL service value system components.
- List the six service value chain activities.
- Describe the purpose of key ITIL practices.
- Distinguish incidents, problems, known errors, workarounds, service requests, and changes.
- Apply continual improvement steps to a scenario.
